Razorpay Buildathon · Problem analysis

India’s revenue recovery gap

Every mature playbook for winning back failed payments is built on a mechanism that Indian regulation does not permit. That single fact decides what is worth building.

01  The constraint that reframes the problem

Start here, because it invalidates the obvious approach.

Stripe’s own retry documentation

Stripe doesn’t retry payments if:
  — No payment methods are available.
  — The issuer returned a hard decline code.
  — The payment card is India-issued.
  — The Stripe Connect account has been disconnected.

Primary  The most sophisticated failed-payment recovery engine in the industry switches itself off for Indian cards.

That is not a product gap. It is the RBI e-mandate regime. A recurring debit in India needs a mandate registered with Additional Factor Authentication, a pre-debit notification ahead of every debit, and an amount inside the mandate’s cap. You cannot quietly re-present a charge eight times over a fortnight and wait for an issuer to relent.

So the dominant recovery mechanism worldwide — the silent retry — is structurally unavailable here as the primary lever. Smart Retries, Adaptive Acceptance, account-updater-driven credential refresh: all of it assumes the merchant can re-attempt without the customer present. Indian recovery has to put the customer back in the loop.

Which changes the question. Not “how do we retry more intelligently?” but “what is the shortest, cheapest, most compliant path back to an authenticated payment moment?” — a UPI intent deep link, a WhatsApp payment link, a mandate re-registration, an eNACH re-presentation timed to a salary credit, or a conversation. That is a decision problem over channels and rails under legal constraint, not a timing-optimisation problem.

On this page’s numbers. Recovery-rate statistics in this market are unusually polluted — a large share of what ranks on search is vendor content repeating vendor claims. Every figure is tagged by how far it could be traced: Primary is quotable, Vendor should be attributed, Unverified is unsafe for a slide, and Check first marks something you must confirm before writing code against it.

An independent verification pass against primary sources found three things wrong or overstated here and they have been corrected in place: the Mastercard retry cadence (unverifiable — removed), the Visa cap (it is category-conditional, not flat), and the scope of the RBI contact-hours window (it is written for calls, not all channels). Corrections are called out where they occur rather than quietly patched.

03  The standards a judge will assume you know

Recovery is a regulated craft with published rules. Getting these wrong is not a missed optimisation, it is a fine.

Soft versus hard declines is the first branch of any agent

Stripe publishes the exact list of decline codes it will not retry. Primary

Non-retryable decline codes
CodeCorrect action
incorrect_numberCollect a new instrument
lost_card · stolen_card · pickup_cardCollect a new instrument; never re-present
revocation_of_authorizationMandate is dead — re-register
revocation_of_all_authorizationsStop all attempts on this customer
authentication_requiredCustomer-present flow with AFA
highest_risk_levelDo not retry; risk review
transaction_not_allowedWrong rail for this instrument

The subtle part: Stripe keeps the retry schedule running for these but only executes once a new payment method appears. Primary The lesson generalises — a hard decline is not a scheduling problem, it is a credential-collection problem, and an agent that treats them alike burns attempts it cannot get back.

Soft declines worth acting on: insufficient funds, generic issuer decline, velocity limits, temporary issuer unavailability, and expired card once refreshed.

Network retry caps are literally why the brief says “stopping rules”

Visa does not publish one flat retry limit. It sorts authorization response codes into four categories — introduced April 2020, with category assignments and code descriptions updated effective 17 April 2021 — and the category decides whether you may retry at all: Primary

Visa Category 1 — “issuer will never approve”

0reattempts permitted Not a scheduling problem. Response Code 14, invalid account number, must never be reattempted using the same account number at all.

Visa Category 2 — “cannot approve at this time”

15reattempts / 30 days The number everyone quotes — but it applies only to this category, and the bulletin does not scope it to card-not-present.

On Mastercard the stop signal is per transaction rather than a cap: Merchant Advice Code 03 means do not try again (for example, account closed) and MAC 21 signals payment cancellation. Mastercard charges a fee for any retry submitted within 30 days of receiving either. Primary

Two corrections worth carrying forward. First, the widely repeated claim that Mastercard caps retries at roughly one per day or ten per thirty days could not be found in any scheme or PSP source — it should not be cited. Second, Visa’s bulletin links excess reattempts to its risk-adjusted fees but states no amount; the familiar “$0.10 per attempt” comes from processor fee schedules (with roughly $0.05 more cross-border), so confirm current pricing with your acquirer rather than quoting it as a scheme rule.

The operative design consequence survives both corrections: a recovery agent needs a per-credential, per-decline-category attempt counter on a rolling 30-day window, plus a hard branch on category and on MAC. Build that and you have most of a defensible stopping-rule story; skip it and a reviewer who works in payments will find it in thirty seconds.

Retry timing: what the state of the art actually does

Stripe Smart Retries is the best-documented engine in the category. Primary

  • Recommended default 8 attempts within 2 weeks; configurable windows of 1 week, 2 weeks, 3 weeks, 1 month or 2 months. Hand-rolled schedules are capped at 3 retries.
  • Named model signals: the number of distinct devices that presented that payment method in the last N hours, and best-time-to-pay effects — debit cards in some countries convert slightly better at 12:01 AM local time.
  • An explicit payment-method fallback order: subscription default method, then subscription default source, then customer default method, then the legacy customer source. Update the wrong field after a failure and retries keep hitting the old instrument.
  • End-of-schedule behaviour is a policy choice, not a default: cancel, mark unpaid, or leave past due.

The most transferable detail for India is how Stripe treats mandate-based rails, which get hard caps rather than ML timing: Primary

Direct-debit retry caps — the precedent for eNACH and UPI AutoPay

Two retries in forty days, for one failure reason. That is what a mandate rail tolerates — and it is the right order of magnitude to assume for eNACH until NPCI documentation says otherwise.

The unglamorous highest-ROI lever

Credential freshness. Visa Account Updater, Mastercard Automatic Billing Updater and network tokenisation quietly repair the dead-credential class of failure before any retry is needed — surfaced as “automatic card updates” by Stripe Primary and Real Time Account Updater by Adyen Vendor. In India, where card-on-file tokenisation is already mandatory, the equivalent hygiene lever is not PAN freshness but mandate health: expiry, remaining cap, VPA validity, revocation state.

Orchestration and cascading

Razorpay Optimizer routes across gateways on roughly 150 parameters against some 600 million payment data points per decision, claiming up to a 10% success-rate improvement with downtime-aware failover and cost-versus-success routing. Vendor Juspay’s Hyperswitch is an open-source orchestrator over 300+ processors, and its Smart Retries feature is explicitly scoped to cases “where user action is not required after entering card information” — non-3DS technical and business declines. Primary

That scoping line matters more than the routing claims: cascading only helps where no customer action is needed, so on AFA-gated and mandate flows it is not the lever. Juspay also ships a Revenue Recovery product — an ML retry engine with 20+ parameters, a configurable retry budget, and error-category gating so you can retry only recoverable classes. Primary It is the closest existing product to this problem statement, and it publishes no numbers.

The dunning ladder

The industry-standard escalation, in order, is remarkably consistent across vendors:

  1. In-app banner or hosted invoice noticeFree, zero annoyance, no consent question
  2. Email with a self-serve update linkCheapest real channel
  3. SMS on a registered templateGate: DLT header and template pre-approval
  4. WhatsApp utility message with a payment linkGate: opt-in on record, utility not marketing category
  5. Failed-payment wall — soft paywall gating the productChurnkey reports this adding 4–12% recovery lift Vendor
  6. Grace-period expiry, then downgrade or suspendThe only rung with real leverage on a consumer
  7. Voice contact or human handoffGate: 08:00–19:00 window, frequency cap, no third-party disclosure
  8. Agency, facilitation council, or legal noticeGate: lender remains liable for agent conduct

FlexPay’s split of “Invisible Recovery” against “Engaged Recovery” is the cleanest published framing of the two tracks. Vendor The arbitrage for an Indian build is simply that track one is mostly closed, so all the competitive energy has to go into track two — which is exactly where the incumbents invest least.

Measurement: where nearly everyone is weak

Gross recovered is not incremental recovered. Attribution can tell you which message preceded a payment; it cannot tell you which payments would have arrived anyway. The only honest answer is a randomised holdout — withhold treatment from an otherwise identical randomly assigned group and difference the outcomes.

This is not a pedantic point. It is the single most common flaw in the category: vendor claims of 41–80% recovery carry no counterfactual, and third-party reviewers flag exactly that. Unverified The brief’s phrase “measured money recovered across a batch” is an invitation to do the honest version.

04  The India layer

Six bodies of rules a recovery agent has to satisfy simultaneously. This is the moat.

RBI Digital Payments E-Mandate Framework, 2026

RBI/DPSS/2026-27/396, issued 21 April 2026 and effective immediately, repealing and consolidating eight circulars issued between August 2019 and August 2024 into one rulebook for recurring transactions on cards, prepaid instruments and UPI. Primary

AFA-free ceiling

₹15,000Per transaction, for recurring e-mandate debits.

Raised ceiling

₹1,00,000Insurance premiums, mutual fund subscriptions, credit-card bill payments.

Pre-transaction notice

24hours minimumNaming merchant, amount, debit date and e-mandate reference.

AFA remains mandatory in four places: mandate registration, modification, withdrawal, and the first transaction — plus any debit above the applicable ceiling. The issuer must also provide a facility, separate from the notification, to opt out of any particular transaction or of the mandate itself, with that opt-out validated by AFA.

The most useful thing the Framework does not say

Three independent full-text reads of the circular found no provision at all on retry, re-presentation, reattempt, or the handling of a failed or declined debit. The E-Mandate Framework is silent on the subject.

That is not permission for unlimited retries — it means the retry constraint lives somewhere else: in NPCI rail rules, in card-scheme rules, or in your payment aggregator’s contract. If you are asked “which regulation caps your retries?”, the honest answer is that RBI does not, and you have to name the actual source. Most write-ups on this subject get it wrong in the confident direction.

What this does to a design. Every debit carries a 24-hour notification lead time and a per-debit customer veto. Retry cadence is therefore measured in days, not minutes. Pause and cancel are first-class customer actions the agent must detect and honour, not edge cases. And an amount above the ceiling is not a retry candidate at all — it is a debit awaiting an authentication click, which is a different state needing a different nudge.

Razorpay documents the eNACH lag explicitly: a retry is attempted only after confirmation or rejection of the last payment, which may take more than 24 hours, with charges shifted to T−1 or T−3 around bank holidays. Primary Any agent that assumes same-day failure feedback on this rail will double-present.

What Razorpay actually gives you — and where it stops

From the Razorpay docs: Primary

  • payment.failed fires on failure or timeout — and is the recommended signal when a customer closes the checkout window or abandons, with payment.authorized watched for late authorisations.
  • On failure the subscription moves to pending, an auto-retry is attempted the following day, and subscription.pending fires.
  • When retries are exhausted the subscription moves to halted; invoices keep generating but are not auto-charged, and the customer is emailed to pay manually.
  • Success at any point fires subscription.charged. The subscription_card_change flag controls whether a customer may update their card at checkout.

The gap, stated plainly. Razorpay’s published recovery policy is “retry tomorrow, then halt, then email.” A fixed schedule, one channel, no per-account strategy, no diagnosis, no measurement. That is not a criticism of a payment gateway — orchestrating recovery is not its job. It is the specification for what you build on top.

Communication compliance — and a common misreading

The instinct is to treat every reminder as needing fresh consent. That is not what the DPDP Act says, and the distinction is worth getting right because it decides whether your agent can legally speak at all.

Processing is lawful on consent (s.4 read with s.6, which confines consent to the purpose stated in the notice) or on one of the enumerated “certain legitimate uses” in s.7. The Act has no GDPR-style “performance of a contract” or “legitimate interests” ground. So a payment reminder to an existing customer needs no fresh consent provided loan servicing and repayment communication was itself a purpose specified in the notice at collection. What the Act prohibits is re-using marketing consent for collections, or servicing consent for promotional content — not servicing messages as such. Unverified

Do not ship on this paragraph alone. The verification pass could not retrieve the verbatim text of s.4, s.6 or s.7 — MeitY was blocked, India Code returned errors, and the Gazette PDF would not extract. The section references above rest on model knowledge, not on retrieved statutory text. Have someone pull the bare Act PDF and read the clause before this goes into compliance copy.

The channel layer is separate. Under TCCCPR 2018 commercial SMS needs a registered header and a pre-approved template, with escalating penalties up to disconnection of telecom resources — but genuine transactional and service-implicit messages to an existing customer are not promotional and are not suppressed by DND preferences. WhatsApp is not telecom SMS, so DLT does not apply, though Meta’s utility-versus-marketing template rules and opt-in requirements do. Unverified

The engineering consequence is concrete either way: never let a generative agent improvise copy on a DLT channel. Template IDs are pre-approved artefacts. The agent selects a template and fills approved variables; it does not write the message.

Collections conduct — and the window is narrower than people think, in one direction only

The governing instrument is RBI/2022-23/108 (DOR.ORG.REC.65/21.04.158/2022-23), Outsourcing of Financial Services — Responsibilities of regulated entities employing Recovery Agents, dated 12 August 2022, still in force as of August 2026 — it is not among the circulars repealed by the Digital Lending Directions, 2025. Primary

It binds commercial banks (excluding payments banks), all-India financial institutions, NBFCs including housing finance companies, co-operative banks and asset reconstruction companies. Microfinance loans sit under a separate Master Direction and are carved out.

The 8-to-7 window is written as a restriction on calling only

The circular prohibits harassment including “persistently calling the borrower and/or calling the borrower before 8:00 a.m. and after 7:00 p.m. for recovery of overdue loans”. SMS and WhatsApp are not covered by that clause — they fall under a separate, time-agnostic prohibition on “sending inappropriate messages either on mobile or through social media”. Email is not expressly named in either, and is caught only by the general bar on intimidation or harassment.

An earlier draft of this page claimed the window covered all four channels alike. It does not. The practical reading is still to hold every channel to 08:00–19:00 as a design default — a 2 a.m. WhatsApp is obviously “inappropriate” and the general harassment clause is broad — but know that the hard, quotable time restriction attaches to voice calls, and that the messaging clause bites on content and persistence instead of the clock.

On liability the circular is unambiguous: “the ultimate responsibility for their outsourced activities vests with them and they are, therefore, responsible for the actions of their service providers” — reinforced for digital lending by the 2025 Directions, under which the regulated entity “shall remain fully responsible and liable for all acts and omissions of the LSP”. An autonomous agent is a service provider. Whatever it says at 9 p.m. is the lender’s liability, not the vendor’s.

Worth borrowing as a frequency default, since RBI gives a window but no contact cap: the US Regulation F “7-in-7” presumption — no more than seven contact attempts per account in seven days, with a cooldown after a conversation. It is the best-codified contact-frequency rule in existence. Unverified

The lever no Western tool has

Section 43B(h), Income Tax Act

Payments to Udyam-registered micro and small enterprises must be made within 15 days absent a written agreement, or by the agreed date subject to an outer cap of 45 days. Miss it and the buyer’s deduction for that expense is disallowed for the year, deferred until payment actually happens. MSMED interest runs at three times the RBI bank rate, compounded monthly, with MSME Samadhaan and the facilitation councils as the adjudication route. Unverified

Effective from 1 April 2024. For a B2B chaser this is a factual, legitimate, non-harassing escalation argument with real financial teeth — and it is unique to India. A fiscal-year-end reminder that non-payment costs the buyer their tax deduction is compliant escalation of a kind no US or EU receivables tool can express.

05  Rails and mandate mechanics

The layer where a recovery agent actually lives. Some of this is well documented; a critical part of it is behind a wall, and it is worth knowing which is which.

The 26-hour in-flight window kills imported dunning logic

For a card e-mandate debit, Stripe waits 26 hours after the payment request before charging — the 24-hour regulatory notification period plus buffer. During that time the PaymentIntent sits in processing and cannot be cancelled. A field, processing.card.customer_notification.completes_at, tells you when the charge will actually be attempted, and approval_requested: true means the customer must authenticate before then. Primary

So the minimum practical retry interval on Indian card standing instructions is roughly one attempt per day. A “retry every six hours” cadence lifted from Western card rails will stack overlapping intents that cannot be recalled, and generate a customer notification per attempt. Whether each retry needs its own fresh 24-hour notification is not stated explicitly anywhere I could reach — but each retry is a new PaymentIntent re-entering the 26-hour window, which implies yes. Treat one-attempt-per-day as the safe default and get it in writing from your aggregator before tightening it.

A ready-made retryability partition

Stripe’s India e-mandate error taxonomy maps cleanly onto the three buckets an agent needs. Mirror these as the core state machine, then map NACH and UPI codes into the same buckets: Primary

Three buckets, not a retry count

india_recurring_payment_mandate_canceledTerminalCancelled, sometimes only discovered at charge time. Full customer-present AFA re-registration — a mandate cannot be updated, only cancelled and recreated.

The legal hinge for out-of-band recovery

On-session collection sits outside the e-mandate debit rules

Stripe notes that while the regulation speaks of “recurring” payments, networks treat any off-session — that is, merchant-initiated — transaction as in scope. The notification and per-debit AFA obligations attach to merchant-initiated debits.

Which implies the lawful post-failure play: a customer-present, customer-authenticated collection — a UPI intent deep link, a UPI collect request, or a hosted payment link where the payer enters their UPI PIN or completes 3DS — is on-session, and therefore outside the 24-hour wait and outside the AutoPay ceiling. So after a failed mandate debit: notify, then invite the customer to settle this cycle on-session, and leave the mandate intact for the next one. Unverified This is an inference from the scope statement, not an explicit RBI clause — verify before it becomes compliance copy. But if it holds, it is the central mechanic of Indian revenue recovery.

What is behind a wall — and what people invent to fill the gap

An operational finding worth recording: npci.org.in returns HTTP 403 to programmatic fetches on both its /PDF/ and /uploads/ paths, while search engines index the same PDFs fine. Every NPCI circular below was located and none could be read. If your agent is meant to keep its own rulebook fresh from circulars, a plain HTTP fetcher will silently fail against the single most important publisher.

Consequently these remain unverified, and the page will not guess at them:

  • NACH / eNACH return reason codes. No code-to-meaning table could be read. Pull these four manually: Circular 240 Reject Reason Codes For E-mandate variants, Circular 274 Change in Return codes and descriptions in NACH, Circular 011 MMS Mandate Variant wise reason code, and NACH-006-FY-24-25 Changes in Rejection Code description in NACH — that last one matters most, because it changed descriptions, so any hardcoded string matching will drift. Also get the subset your own sponsor bank actually returns; acquirers pass through subsets and sometimes remap.
  • Presentation and re-presentation limits, settlement cycles, holiday behaviour. The authoritative document is the NACH Procedural Guidelines V.6. Note also that Circular 016 FY2019-20 is titled, in part, “Penalty on specific returns” — meaning some retries cost money per attempt, not just goodwill. An agent that maximises attempts without knowing which return codes carry penalties can be net-negative on revenue.
  • The UPI AutoPay retry cap. The commonly cited “1 original debit plus 3 retries” figure traces, in this research, only to a Razorpay marketing blog post — not to the NPCI circular and not to Razorpay’s own docs. Do not hardcode three retries on the strength of that. The current primary source is UPI OC No. 223 FY 2025-26, Enhancement of UPI AutoPay.
  • UPI AutoPay mandate-execution failure codes. No primary listing found. Source them from your PSP’s live API reference, and log raw codes verbatim from day one so the mapping can be built empirically before the spec is in hand.

One UPI ceiling is documented: per Stripe, UPI does not support recurring transactions above ₹15,000 — stated as a flat limitation rather than merely an AFA trigger. Unverified NPCI has revised AutoPay limits at least twice by circular (OC-151, OC-151A), and the figure is category-dependent, so treat it as configuration read from a dated source, never a constant in code. A debit above the cap fails structurally and no retry policy rescues it; the recovery path is an on-session collect or a different rail entirely.

07  Where the whitespace actually is

Eight gaps, ordered by how directly the brief points at them.

Nobody spans the funnel

A customer who abandons checkout, then fails a mandate, then goes sixty days overdue is handled by three unrelated systems with no shared memory, no shared contact history, and no shared sense of how much this person has already been bothered.

Diagnosis barely exists

Incumbents act on decline codes. The brief says “payment degradation → root cause → recovery action” — and issuer degradation, gateway downtime, mandate revocation, genuine insufficient funds and intent-to-churn all demand different actions. That middle step is commercially unclaimed.

Intervention is a static ladder

Standard practice is a fixed dunning sequence. Missing: per-account choice of channel, timing and offer by expected value net of cost, annoyance and legal constraint. Uplift modelling is routine in marketing and rare here.

Measurement is self-serving

Vendors report gross recovered. Almost nobody runs a holdout. An honest incremental number is both harder to produce and more persuasive to anyone who knows the field.

Compliance is bolted on

No incumbent models scheme caps, mandate ceilings, notification lead times, contact windows, consent basis, template category and annoyance budget as one constraint set that every planned action must clear before execution.

Audit trails are logs, not justifications

A regulated lender has to answer “why this person, at this hour, on this channel, with this offer, on what consent basis?” Nobody exposes that as a queryable artefact.

India-native blind spots

No silent-retry lever. UPI intent as the highest-converting recovery rail, largely unexploited by the recovery-tool category. Hinglish and vernacular voice. eNACH banking calendars. Section 43B(h) as legitimate leverage.

Promise-to-pay is untracked

The standard collections KPI — a captured commitment, then kept or broken — is essentially absent from the Indian SME receivables stack, despite being the single best predictor of whether cash arrives.

Nine conditions, all cheap to evaluate, all auditable. Together they are the difference between a demo that recovers money and a demo that would get its operator fined.

09  The Razorpay build surface

What is actually wireable in test mode, what needs a sales conversation, and the one loop that will run live on stage.

Razorpay ships an official MCP server

This is the single biggest time-saver available and it is easy to miss: github.com/razorpay/razorpay-mcp-server (Go), runnable three ways — remotely via npx mcp-remote https://mcp.razorpay.com/mcp with a base64 key_id:key_secret header, locally via docker run razorpay/mcp, or built from source. It exposes 40+ tools, so the intervention APIs arrive already tool-shaped. Primary

Confirmed tools relevant to recovery: create_payment_link, create_payment_link_upi, send_payment_link, update_payment_link, capture_payment, initiate_payment, resend_otp, submit_otp, fetch_payment_card_details, create_qr_code, create_refund, fetch_tokens, revoke_token, plus order and settlement tools.

Note what is absent: no subscription tools — no charge-invoice, no update-card — and no payout creation. Those legs must go through the REST API or an official SDK. Plan for MCP plus one SDK rather than MCP alone.

The subscription failure ladder, precisely

More specific than the general docs suggest: a failed day-0 auto-charge moves the subscription to pending, and Razorpay reattempts on days 1, 2 and 3; if all fail it moves to halted. Primary That is the four-day window the agent operates inside — and it should act while pending, before Razorpay’s own retries are exhausted, not after halting.

Best play while pending

Just deliver a card-update link. If the customer changes their card while pending, Razorpay automatically charges the last invoice and returns the subscription to active on success. No custom charge orchestration needed.

Best play once halted

Invoices keep being created but are not auto-charged. Enumerate invoices in issued state and charge them manually. This is the difference between “churned” and “recovered”.

A hard constraint on the action space. Subscriptions can only be updated in authenticated and active states — the Update Subscription API is closed in created, pending and halted. Which is to say: closed in exactly the states a recovery agent cares about. The available moves are card-update links, manual invoice charges and payment links, not subscription mutation.

Free money, and the easiest thing to demo

payment.authorized fires when a payment is authorized but not captured, and Razorpay auto-refunds uncaptured authorizations after a window. An agent that watches for it and calls capture_payment in time recovers revenue with zero customer contact — no consent question, no template, no contact window. It is also trivially demonstrable in test mode.

Test mode: what works, what stalls

  • Webhooks are per-mode and a test-mode webhook receives only test events — so the whole agent can be driven end to end through a tunnel URL without a live merchant account. Primary
  • Confirmed event strings: payment.failed, payment.authorized, order.paid. Primary
  • Unconfirmed event strings — the supported-events page could not be fetched, twice. The subscription.*, invoice.*, payment_link.*, refund.* and dispute namespaces are from memory, not a retrieved page. Check first subscription.pending and subscription.halted are the agent’s two most important triggers, and a wrong string is a silently dead subscription — confirm them before coding.
  • Subscriptions have a documented test page, so you can manufacture pending and halted rather than waiting for real failures. This removes the main data-availability risk. Primary
  • Smart Collect has a test-payments flow — so the “offer an alternative rail” branch is demoable: create a virtual account, simulate an inbound transfer, show the agent reconciling it against the failed order.
  • RazorpayX test mode stalls. Dummy balance, no approval workflow, and payouts must be advanced manually from the Dashboard. Do not build an automated end-to-end payout demo. Live payouts additionally need activation and KYC.
  • Optimizer and Magic Checkout gating is unconfirmed. Both exist with docs, but no statement on self-serve versus enterprise enablement was retrievable. Optimizer is conceptually the strongest recovery lever here — verify you can actually demo it before putting it in the pitch. Check first

The one loop with no dependencies

Test-mode webhook → payment.failed or subscription.pending → agent diagnoses and decides → create_payment_link_upi + send_payment_link (or a manual invoice charge for halted subscriptions) → payment_link.paid / order.paid closes the loop and stops the nudging.

Every leg is test-mode capable, MCP-covered, and free of enterprise gating, KYC and manual Dashboard steps. If something has to work live in front of judges, it is this.

10  What the research implies for a build

Implications, not a design. The design should come from whichever surfaces you pick.

Model it as a constrained sequential decision problem

State is the obligation plus its failure history, mandate health, consent state, contactability and accumulated annoyance. Actions span retry now, retry at a chosen time, cascade to an alternate rail, UPI intent link, WhatsApp payment link, SMS on a template, voice, mandate re-registration, an instalment or partial offer, human handoff, and stop. The objective is rupees recovered minus cost minus annoyance and churn risk — and the constraint set is non-negotiable rather than a penalty term.

Report the number that is hard to fake

Randomise a holdout at account level — 15–20% of the batch, stratified by amount and decline family — and report gross recovered, incremental recovered, recovery rate by decline family, days-to-cash, contacts per recovery, cost per rupee recovered, and constraint violations, which should be zero. On surface D add the standard collections set: DSO, average days delinquent, collections effectiveness index, aging roll rates and promise-to-pay kept rate.

Pick two surfaces, not four

Option A · Payment failure + mandate recovery

Seed from Razorpay test-mode payment.failed and subscription.pending, and act inside the day-1-to-3 retry window rather than after halting. Strongest technical story, cleanest measurement, fully demoable per section 09, and the India constraint becomes the headline insight rather than an obstacle.

Option B · B2B receivables + promise-to-pay

Richest compliant escalation ladder, the unique Section 43B(h) and MSMED leverage, and promise-to-pay as a first-class tracked object. Weaker real-time signal, stronger narrative.

One caution on scope. Hinglish voice recovery is the most demonstrable direction and the most legally loaded — contact windows, bot disclosure, recording consent, and lender liability for agent conduct all land on it at once. It makes a superb thirty-second demo and a difficult compliance story. If you build it, build the gate first.

11  Build status

The research became a running system on 28–29 August. This section tracks what is built against what sections 08–10 demanded, and is updated as slices land. The working tracker lives in the repository as docs/PROGRESS.md.

Shipped and verified

A Python/FastAPI backend with the PSP isolated behind an adapter (Razorpay webhook with HMAC verification and redelivery dedupe; a seeded synthetic generator emitting the identical canonical event). MySQL with the session timezone pinned to UTC at connect — seventeen tables and six views, including the decision spine: decisions → decision_candidates → gate_evaluations. And the core loop the brief asked for: deterministic diagnosis across ten failure families, candidate enumeration, ten constraint gates that each record the authority they enforce, and a transparent expected-value score in integer paise — hand-written priors and an explicit annoyance cost, deliberately not a model.

The first live batch, in numbers

502 events → 502 decisions, converged on re-run. Voice chosen 131 times, re-presentation 99, mandate re-registration 98, UPI intent link 74, human escalation 26 — and 74 deliberately withheld as the 15% randomised control arm. 281 candidate actions blocked across 10,544 gate evaluations. Constraint violations: zero, checked by a view that must stay empty.

The audit rows say what they don't know

Where a cap could not be primary-sourced — the NPCI retry limits section 04 flags — the gate's audit row carries the admission verbatim: caps.yaml:retry.upi_autopay [UNVERIFIED]. The expected-value contrast landed as designed: voice is EV-negative on a ₹499 subscription and wins on a ₹45,000 EMI, because customer irritation is priced as an explicit line item, not assumed away.

Feature-complete, 1 September

The execution slice runs exactly-once on an idempotency key and creates real Razorpay test-mode payment links; a deterministic simulator advances outcomes so the batch shows ₹7.3L recovered with ₹2.5L incremental over the holdout (19.2% treatment vs 11.7% control). The Hinglish voice agent — one deterministic brain behind both a text simulation and a LiveKit-plus-Sarvam live-audio worker — screens every drafted line before it is spoken, logs drafted-versus-spoken per utterance, and captures promises with a spoken read-back: the first recorded promise resolved kept. Constraint violations remain zero. Left for demo day: a captured real test-mode webhook, the script, and a wifi-off rehearsal.
